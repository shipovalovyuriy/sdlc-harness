# Anti-Patterns

## Optimizing Non-Constraint

Improving a part that is not limiting throughput. This creates local wins and global stagnation.

## Increasing Inflow Into A Blocked System

Adding ideas, tickets, PRs, meetings, or people before increasing outflow through the constraint.

## Maximizing Utilization Instead Of Throughput

Keeping everyone busy increases queues, context switching, lead time, and fragility.

## Treating Symptoms Instead Of Stock-Flow Structure

Fixing the visible pain without changing the accumulation, inflow, outflow, feedback loop, delay, or policy that creates it.

## Adding Process Before Removing Queue

Adding ceremonies, approvals, dashboards, or governance while the real queue remains blocked.

## Confusing Activity With Progress

Counting meetings, tickets closed, story points, documents, or commits instead of valuable throughput.

## Solving With More People When Constraint Is Decision Latency

Adding capacity increases coordination load when the real bottleneck is approval, ownership, prioritization, or unclear decision rights.

## Ignoring Delays

Overreacting to late signals or abandoning good interventions before their effects appear.

## Moving Constraint Accidentally

Fixing one queue without predicting where work will pile up next.

## Overengineering The Intervention

Creating a large transformation when a small WIP limit, batch-size reduction, decision owner, or feedback-loop change would test the leverage point.
